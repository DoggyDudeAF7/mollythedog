Getting Help

Know what to do when something goes wrong online — and when to ask somebody else for help.





↓Scroll Down↓

🆘

When Something Goes Wrong

Cyber safety is not only about preventing problems. It is also about knowing what to do if something has already happened.


If an account is hacked, somebody is harassing you, private information is shared or you fall for a scam, acting quickly can often reduce the damage.


You do not need to deal with a serious online problem by yourself.

💬

Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying can include threatening, humiliating, aggressive or repeatedly unwanted behaviour through messages, games, social media or other online services.


It can involve insults, rumours, fake accounts, embarrassing posts, deliberate exclusion or repeated unwanted messages.


If it is happening to you, avoid responding angrily. Save evidence, block the person where appropriate and tell somebody you trust.

📸

Save Evidence

If something serious happens online, evidence can help show exactly what occurred.


Useful evidence can include screenshots, usernames, profile links, messages, dates, times and website addresses.


If blocking somebody will cause their messages to disappear, save important evidence first when it is safe to do so.

🚫

Block

Most messaging services, games and social networks allow you to block another account.


Blocking can prevent the person from directly contacting you, viewing some parts of your account or continuing unwanted communication.


Blocking is useful when somebody is harassing you, repeatedly contacting you or making you uncomfortable.

🚩

Report

Reporting sends information about an account, message or post to the platform so it can be reviewed.


Reporting may be appropriate for harassment, threats, scams, impersonation, dangerous behaviour or content that breaks the platform's rules.


Reporting and blocking are different: blocking protects your own account, while reporting alerts the service to the behaviour.

🧑‍🏫

Tell a Trusted Person

Serious online problems can be easier to solve when another person knows what is happening.


You could speak with a parent, guardian, teacher, school wellbeing staff member or another trusted adult.


Explain what happened clearly and show them any screenshots, usernames or other evidence you have saved.

🎣

If You Fell for a Scam

People can fall for convincing scams. What matters next is responding quickly.


Stop communicating with the scammer, save evidence and change any passwords you entered into a suspicious website.


If money or payment details were involved, tell a trusted adult and contact the relevant bank, payment provider or service as soon as possible.

🔓

If Your Account Was Hacked

If somebody else has accessed your account, try to regain control as quickly as possible.


Change the password, sign out unfamiliar devices, check your recovery information and enable two-factor authentication.


Also look for messages, posts or account changes you did not make. If you cannot sign in, use the service's official account recovery system.

🪪

If Private Information Was Shared

If you accidentally post private information, remove it as quickly as possible where you can.


Remember that another person may already have copied or screenshotted it, so deleting the original does not always remove every copy.


If the information includes passwords, account recovery details, payment information or something that could put you at risk, tell a trusted adult and take action immediately.

📱

If You Lose a Device

A lost phone, tablet or laptop may give somebody access to your personal information if the device is not properly protected.


Use any available device-finding service, tell a trusted adult and secure important accounts if you think somebody else may have accessed the device.


A strong screen lock and automatic device locking can greatly reduce the risk before a device is ever lost.

⚡

When You Should Act Quickly

Some situations need a faster response than others.


Act quickly if somebody has your password, payment information has been exposed, your account is being used by somebody else or a threat involves your safety.


Do not wait simply because you are worried about getting into trouble for clicking something or making a mistake.

What Should I Do?


Problem First Step What to Do Next
Cyberbullying Save evidence Block, report and tell somebody
Suspicious account login Change your password Check logged-in devices and enable 2FA
Scam message Stop responding Block, report and verify any claims independently
Password accidentally shared Change it immediately Check the account and enable 2FA
Private information posted Remove it where possible Tell a trusted adult if the information is sensitive
Unknown program installed Stop using it Ask for technical help and check the device
Money lost to a scam Tell a trusted adult Contact the relevant bank or payment provider
🛡️

Remember

Cyber safety is not about being scared to use the internet. It is about understanding risks, recognising warning signs and knowing how to respond.


Think before you click, protect your accounts and ask for help when something does not seem right.
