Getting Help

Sometimes problems happen even when you have been careful.

If something online makes you uncomfortable, threatens you, affects your account or puts your information at risk, there are steps you can take.

💬

Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying can include repeated harmful, humiliating, threatening or unwanted behaviour through messages, social media, games or other online services.

It can include:

  • Threatening messages.
  • Repeated insults.
  • Spreading rumours.
  • Sharing embarrassing material.
  • Creating fake accounts.
  • Deliberately excluding or targeting somebody.

What To Do About Cyberbullying

  1. Do not respond angrily. Responding can sometimes make the situation worse.
  2. Save evidence. Keep screenshots, usernames, messages, links and dates.
  3. Block the account. This can stop further direct contact.
  4. Report the behaviour. Most websites and games provide reporting tools.
  5. Tell somebody. Speak to a trusted adult, parent, guardian or teacher.
📸

Save Evidence

If something serious happens online, evidence can help explain exactly what happened.

Useful evidence can include:

  • Screenshots.
  • Usernames.
  • Profile links.
  • Messages.
  • Email addresses.
  • Dates and times.
  • Website addresses.

Save important evidence before blocking an account if doing so would make the messages disappear.

🚫

Block & Report

Blocking usually prevents another account from directly contacting you.

Reporting sends information about the behaviour to the platform so that it can be reviewed.

Reporting is especially important for threats, harassment, scams, impersonation and dangerous behaviour.

🧑‍🏫

Tell a Trusted Person

Serious online problems can be much easier to deal with when another person knows what is happening.

You could speak with:

  • A parent or guardian.
  • A teacher.
  • A school wellbeing staff member.
  • Another trusted adult.

Explain what happened and show them any evidence you have saved.

💳

If You Fall for a Scam

Acting quickly can help reduce the damage.

  1. Stop communicating with the scammer.
  2. Change passwords if you entered them.
  3. Tell a trusted adult.
  4. Contact the relevant service or company.
  5. If payment information was involved, tell the appropriate bank or payment provider.
  6. Save screenshots and other evidence.
🔓

If Your Account Is Taken Over

  1. Try to change your password.
  2. Use account recovery tools.
  3. Sign out unfamiliar devices.
  4. Check recovery details.
  5. Enable two-factor authentication.
  6. Tell people if strange messages were sent from you.
📋

What Should I Do?

Suspicious login Change password Check logged-in devices

🛡️

Remember

Cyber safety is not about avoiding the internet.

It is about understanding risks, recognising warning signs and knowing what to do when something does not seem right.
