Online Safety

Think before you click. Protect your privacy. Know who and what you can trust online.

Staying Safe Online

The internet is useful for learning, gaming, communicating, watching videos and creating things, but not everything online is trustworthy.


Cyber safety means thinking carefully about what you click, what you share, who you communicate with and whether the information you see can be trusted.

🎣

Scams & Phishing

Phishing is when somebody pretends to be a trusted company, organisation or person to trick you into giving them information or taking an unsafe action.


They may ask for passwords, payment details, verification codes or personal information. Some scams also try to convince you to download a file or send money.


Be suspicious of urgent warnings, unexpected prizes, strange links and messages asking for information that a real company should not need.

⚠️

Watch for Pressure Tactics

Scammers often try to stop you from thinking carefully by making something sound extremely urgent.


Messages such as “Your account will be deleted in 10 minutes” or “You must pay immediately” are designed to make you panic and act before checking whether the message is real.


Slow down, check the information independently and do not let a message pressure you into making a quick decision.

🔗

Think Before You Click

A link can appear trustworthy while sending you somewhere completely different. Fake websites can also closely copy the design of real services.


Before entering a password or personal information, check the website address carefully. If a message claims to be from a company, open the official app or type the website address yourself instead of using the message link.

👁️

Protect Your Privacy

Privacy means having control over who can access information about you.


Avoid publicly sharing information such as your home address, private phone number, exact location, account details or passwords.


Also check who can see your posts. A private account can reduce who sees your content, but anything shared online can still be copied or screenshotted.

📍

Location Sharing

Apps can use your location for useful features such as maps, transport and weather, but not every app needs permanent access.


Be careful when sharing your live location and check whether photos or posts reveal where you live, study or regularly spend time.


Review app permissions occasionally and remove location access from apps that no longer need it.

💬

Social Media

Social media can make information spread extremely quickly. A photo, comment or video can be copied and shared far beyond the people you originally intended to see it.


Before posting, think about who can see it, whether it reveals private information and whether you would still be comfortable with somebody seeing it later.

🎮

Online Gaming

Multiplayer games allow you to communicate with people around the world, but somebody you meet in a game is still somebody you only know through the internet.


Do not reveal private information in game chat, never give another player your password and be suspicious of offers for free currency, items or account upgrades.


Use mute, block and report tools if another player behaves inappropriately or makes you uncomfortable.

👣

Your Digital Footprint

Your digital footprint is the trail of information created by your activity online.


Posts, comments, photos, videos, usernames and accounts can remain online for a long time. Even deleted information may have already been copied or archived.


Before posting something, consider whether you would still be comfortable with other people seeing it in the future.
