Online Safety

The internet lets us communicate, play games, share ideas, watch videos, learn new things and connect with people around the world.

But not everything online is what it appears to be. Understanding scams, privacy, online communication and the information you share can help you avoid many common problems.

Scams & Phishing

Phishing happens when somebody pretends to be a trusted person, company or organisation in order to trick you.

They may try to steal your password, payment information, verification codes or other private information.

Warning signs

  • Messages telling you to act immediately.
  • Unexpected prizes or giveaways.
  • Requests for passwords.
  • Strange website addresses.
  • Unexpected attachments.
  • Requests for verification codes.
  • Messages that create panic or fear.

A Typical Phishing Message

“Your account has been locked. Click here within 20 minutes or your account will be permanently deleted.”

The message is designed to make you panic.

Instead of clicking the supplied link, open the official app or type the company's real website address yourself.

🔗

Think Before You Click

A link can display one address while actually sending you somewhere completely different.

Fake websites can also closely copy real websites, including their logos, colours and login screens.

Before clicking

  • Check who sent the message.
  • Read the website address carefully.
  • Do not rush because a message says it is urgent.
  • Be cautious with shortened URLs.
  • Do not enter passwords into suspicious pages.
👁️

Online Privacy

Privacy means having control over who can access information about you.

Information that appears harmless by itself can sometimes reveal much more when it is combined with other information.

Be careful when sharing

  • Your home address.
  • Your exact location.
  • Your phone number.
  • Your school or regular routine.
  • Private photos.
  • Travel plans.
  • Personal account information.
📍

Location Sharing

Apps can use your location for maps, transport, weather, games and other useful features.

However, not every app needs permanent access to your location.

  • Check which apps can access your location.
  • Avoid sharing your live location publicly.
  • Consider whether a photo reveals where you are.
  • Review location permissions occasionally.
  • Avoid publicly sharing your regular routine.
💬

Social Media

Social media can make information spread very quickly.

Even if you delete something later, somebody may already have saved, downloaded or screenshotted it.

Before posting, ask yourself:

  1. Who can see this?
  2. Does this reveal private information?
  3. Could somebody misunderstand this?
  4. Would I be comfortable if someone saved it?
  5. Would I still be happy with this post next year?
🎮

Online Gaming

Multiplayer games let people from different places play and communicate together.

However, somebody you meet in a game is still somebody you only know through the internet.

Gaming safety

  • Do not reveal private information in chat.
  • Never give another player your password.
  • Be suspicious of “free currency” offers.
  • Avoid unknown cheats and downloads.
  • Use mute, block and report tools when needed.
  • Be cautious if somebody asks you to move the conversation to another app.
👣

Your Digital Footprint

A digital footprint is the trail of information created by your activity online.

It can include posts, comments, videos, photos, accounts, usernames and other information.

Information posted online may remain accessible for a very long time, even if the original copy is later removed.
