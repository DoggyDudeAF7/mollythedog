Passwords & Security

Protect your accounts. Protect your devices. Make it harder for somebody else to get in.





↓Scroll Down↓

🔑

Strong Passwords

A password is one of the main barriers stopping somebody else from accessing your account.


Weak passwords are often short, common or based on information such as names, birthdays and simple patterns.


A stronger password should be long, unique and difficult for another person to guess.

📏

Why Length Matters

Longer passwords generally have many more possible combinations than shorter passwords.


That makes them harder to guess or crack. A long passphrase can also be easier to remember than a short collection of random characters.


Avoid making passwords longer simply by repeating obvious patterns such as 123456789.

🧩

Passphrases

A passphrase is a password made from several words or parts joined together.


Something like Blue-River!Train-Window-82 is far longer and less predictable than a common password such as password123.


Avoid famous quotes, song lyrics and obvious phrases that other people may be able to predict.

♻️

Avoid Password Reuse

Using the same password on many websites creates a larger security risk.


If one service suffers a data breach, attackers may try the exposed email address and password on other websites.


Important accounts, especially your email account, should use unique passwords.

🗝️

Password Managers

A password manager can securely store passwords so you do not need to remember every password yourself.


This makes it easier to use long, unique passwords for different accounts instead of reusing one password everywhere.


The main password protecting the password manager should itself be strong and kept private.

📲

Two-Factor Authentication

Two-factor authentication, usually called 2FA, adds another security check after your password.


This may involve an authenticator app, a security key, a temporary code or an approval notification on another device.


Even if somebody discovers your password, 2FA can make it much harder for them to access your account.

🔢

Keep Verification Codes Private

Login verification codes are often used to prove that the person signing in actually owns the account.


If another person asks you to send them one of these codes, they may already have your password and be trying to finish the login.


Treat verification codes like passwords: do not give them to anybody else.

🦠

Malware

Malware is software designed to perform harmful or unwanted actions on a device.


It may steal information, monitor activity, damage files, display unwanted advertising or allow somebody else to control parts of the device.


Malware can sometimes be hidden in fake programs, suspicious attachments, pirated software or unknown downloads.

📥

Safe Downloads

Download software from trusted sources whenever possible.


Be careful with random download websites, unknown email attachments, cracked programs and suspicious game cheats.


If your browser or operating system warns that a file may be unsafe, do not ignore the warning unless you understand why it appeared.

⬆️

Keep Software Updated

Updates are not only for new features. They can also fix security problems discovered after software has been released.


Keeping your browser, apps and operating system updated reduces the chance that known security weaknesses can be used against your device.

💻

Protect Your Devices

Phones, tablets and computers often contain access to messages, photos, documents and many of your online accounts.


Use a secure PIN or password, enable automatic locking, install updates and keep important files backed up.


Do not leave an unlocked device unattended where somebody else could access it.

📡

Public Wi-Fi

Public Wi-Fi can be convenient, but you should still be cautious when connecting to networks you do not control.


Make sure you are connecting to the correct network and avoid ignoring browser security warnings.


When you have finished using a public network, you can also tell your device to forget it so it does not automatically reconnect later.

🚨

Signs an Account May Be Compromised

Unexpected activity can sometimes be a warning that somebody else has accessed your account.


Look for unfamiliar login alerts, messages you did not send, password changes you did not make, unknown devices or changes to your recovery information.


If something looks wrong, act quickly rather than waiting to see what happens.
